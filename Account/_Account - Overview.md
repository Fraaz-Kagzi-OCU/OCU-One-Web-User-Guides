# Account — Overview

**Account** covers the personal settings and small conveniences tied to your own login, rather than to any one job, project, or record. It's a short list — a profile page, a layout preference, and how one-off tips behave — but each piece is easy to miss if you don't know where to look.

## The pieces

**My Account** is your personal profile page — your date of birth, the page you land on when you log in, and your profile picture. Your name is shown there too, but it's set by an administrator, not by you.

The **Sidebar** is the panel that runs down the left of a record's tabs (a job, a client, a user, and so on). Collapsing or expanding it is a display preference, not something tied to any particular record.

**Onboarding tips** are small pop-ups OCU One shows the first time you encounter a feature you might not have found on your own — for example, a tip about adding more columns the first time you open a table view.

## Why this matters

None of these settings affect anyone but you — they're about making OCU One fit the way you personally work:

- **Your profile** — keeping your date of birth, default landing page, and picture up to date.
- **Your layout** — how much screen space the sidebar takes up while you work, remembered across sessions.
- **Your onboarding** — once you've seen and dismissed a tip, it's gone for good, freeing up space for tips you haven't seen yet.

## The guides

[Updating your account profile](Updating%20your%20account%20profile.md) covers My Account end to end — setting your date of birth, choosing your default home page, and adding or removing a profile picture.

[Collapsing or expanding the sidebar](Collapsing%20or%20expanding%20the%20sidebar.md) covers toggling the sidebar on any record's page, and how your choice is remembered the next time you open one.

[Dismissing an onboarding tip](Dismissing%20an%20onboarding%20tip.md) covers clicking past a tip once you've seen it, and confirms it's gone for good rather than reappearing next session.

These three aren't a sequence — there's no "start here and work through it" flow. Each is a standalone setting you'll likely only touch once in a while.
